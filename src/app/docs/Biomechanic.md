Here’s the paper in plain language, with focus on the **scoring (grading) system** and what “good” joint angles roughly looked like.

***

## 1. How they graded each cover drive

For every batter, they filmed the cover drive three times and had **three judges** score the technique. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)

- The stroke was broken into **two phases**, each with **0–10 points**:  
  1. **Phase I – Back‑lift & Placement of the Foot** (setup and stride)  
  2. **Phase II – Downswing & Impact (Contact)** (actual swing and ball contact) [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
- So each trial had a maximum of **20 points total** (10 + 10). [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
- Each judge gave a score for both phases in each of the three trials; the researchers then:
  - Picked the **best trial** for each player.
  - Took the **average of the three judges’ scores** on that trial as the final “performance score” for the cover drive. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)

That final 0–20 score is what they correlated with joint angles.

***

## 2. What angles they measured (and how)

They used a **Canon 70D (30 fps)** from the side (sagittal plane), drew stick figures, and used **Kinovea** to measure angles. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)

They measured angles at these joints on the **right‑handed batter’s body**: [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)

- Right & left **ankle**
- Right & left **knee**
- Right & left **hip**
- Right & left **shoulder**
- Right & left **elbow**
- Right & left **wrist**

And they did that at **two key instants**:

1. **Placement of the front foot** (just after the stride lands)  
2. **Execution / contact** (when the bat would contact the ball) [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)

***

## 3. Which variables actually correlated with better drives

They used **Pearson correlation** between joint angles and the judges’ cover‑drive scores. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)

With only **5 players**, the statistical threshold was very high, so only a few angles showed “significant” relationships:

- At **foot placement (Phase I)**  
  - **Left knee angle** had a **strong negative correlation** with performance (r = −0.970, p < 0.01). [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
    - Interpretation from authors: **if the left knee angle gets bigger, performance gets worse.** [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
    - Bigger angle = knee more straight; smaller angle = more bend.  
    - So *more bend in the front knee at landing* (smaller angle) was associated with better cover drives in their sample.

- At **execution / contact (Phase II)**  
  - **Right hip angle** had a **strong positive correlation** (r = 0.897, p < 0.05). [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
  - **Left elbow angle** also had a **strong positive correlation** (r = 0.902, p < 0.05). [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
    - Interpretation: **larger angles at the right hip and left elbow at contact were associated with better drives** in this small group. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
    - Larger hip angle = more “open” hip or bigger thigh‑torso angle; larger left‑elbow angle = straighter front arm.

So in simple cricket language:

- **Good players (in this study) tended to:**
  - Land with a **nicely flexed front knee**, not a stiff, straight front leg. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
  - At impact, have a **more open right hip** (good hip clearance/rotation) and a **more extended front arm (left elbow)**, giving a strong, long hitting lever. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)

## 4. Rough “benchmark” angles from their table

These are **average angles** (mean ± SD) from their five state‑level female players, not universal standards, but they give ballpark numbers. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)

### At placement of the foot (Phase I)

- **Left knee angle:** about **153° ± 8°**, and performance was better when this angle was **smaller** (more knee bend). [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
- **Right hip angle:** about **161° ± 5°**. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)

### At execution/impact (Phase II)

- **Left knee angle:** about **121° ± 6°**, i.e., clearly bent front knee at contact. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
- **Right hip angle:** about **171° ± 7°**, i.e., more “opened out” hip at contact; higher angle = better scores in this study. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
- **Left elbow angle:** about **106° ± 29°**, with better performance as this angle **increased** (straighter front arm). [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)

You *cannot* treat these as hard “ideal values”, but you can say:

- “In one small study of state‑level women batters, better cover drives tended to show:  
  - a **bent front knee** (around 120° at impact),  
  - a **well‑opened front‑side hip** (around 170°), and  
  - a **fairly extended front elbow** (angle > 100°).” [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)

## 5. How you can map this to your grading logic

For your tool, you can translate their findings into a **soft scoring heuristic**, e.g.:

- **Front knee (left knee) at impact:**  
  - Reward angles around **110–130°** (clearly flexed) and down‑weight very straight (>150°) or excessively collapsed (<100°) knees, noting that the paper saw better scores with more flex. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
- **Right hip at impact:**  
  - Reward larger angles (e.g., **160–180°**) as indicating good hip clearance / opening during the drive. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
- **Left elbow at impact:**  
  - Reward relatively **large elbow angles** (straighter arm) vs very bent elbows, echoing their positive correlation. [espncricinfo](https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/cricket-s-turning-points-the-15-degree-rule-525937)
